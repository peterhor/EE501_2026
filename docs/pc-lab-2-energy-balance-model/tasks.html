Reference values and the model

  • As provided: solar constant S0 = 1361 W m⁻², planetary albedo αp = 0.30, atmospheric emissivity εa = 0.5, Stefan–Boltzmann constant σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴. Selecting an exercise in the lab restores whatever settings that exercise needs.

The whole lab rests on one small model, in six equations (the lab's Recap shows the same, with the terms coloured to match its flux diagram):

¼(1−αp)S0 = (1−εa)σTs4 + εaσTa4(1)

Energy in = energy out at the top of the atmosphere (Goosse et al. 2010, Fig. 2.5): absorbed sunlight on the left; on the right, surface infrared that passes through the layer plus what the layer radiates to space. Together they equal σTe4.

εaσTs4 = 2εaσTa4  →  Ta4 = ½Ts4(2)

The layer's own balance. It absorbs εaσTs4 and radiates εaσTa4 both up and down; by Kirchhoff's law the same εa sets both.

Ts = {¼(1−αp)S0 / [(1−εa)σ + ½εaσ]}¼(3)

Solve for the surface by putting (2) into (1): Ts follows from S0, αp and εa alone.

Ta = (½)¼ Ts ≈ 0.841 Ts(4)

The atmosphere follows the surface (Goosse et al. 2010, eq. 2.13).

(1−εa)σTs4 + εaσTa4 = σTs4 − εaσ(Ts4 − Ta4)(5)

The outgoing side of (1), rearranged: what a bare surface would radiate, minus the part the atmosphere holds back.

GHE = εaσ(Ts4 − Ta4)(6)

That held-back part is a measure of the greenhouse effect, in W m⁻².

IntroOne planet, one balance

The simplest climate model of the Earth rests on one assumption: the planet's energy budget is in balance, so the energy coming in equals the energy going out. At the top of the atmosphere, the absorbed sunlight ¼(1−αp)S0 must equal the outgoing infrared radiation, (1−εa)σTs4 + εaσTa4 — the part of the surface's own infrared that passes straight through the atmosphere, plus what the atmosphere itself radiates to space. A single atmospheric layer absorbs a fraction εa of the infrared coming up from the surface and radiates εaσTa4 both upward and downward.

E3What drives the greenhouse effect

The greenhouse effect is GHE = εaσ(Ts4 − Ta4), equation (6) above: it depends on the emissivity of the atmosphere and on how much colder the atmosphere is than the surface. Define Tdiff = Ts − Ta. The plot traces Tdiff against GHE along the same εa sweep of 0.0 → 1.0. Predict the shape of that curve, then reveal it.

  1. How does Tdiff vary with different values of GHE? When is the GHE largest, and how do you explain this?
  2. Where in the atmosphere (at what height) is the greenhouse effect of CO₂ and other greenhouse gases most effective? Explain your answer.

E6Other planets

The same energy balance works elsewhere in the solar system. Mercury, the closest planet to the sun, has virtually no atmosphere (εa ≈ 0), an albedo of 0.10 and S0 = 9160 W m⁻². Mars has a very thin atmosphere, an albedo of 0.25 and S0 = 580 W m⁻². Once all four are in, the readings come back, and the lab checks each estimate against the model as you set that case up. The estimates are not marked.   3. What is the approximate average surface temperature Ts of Mars?
