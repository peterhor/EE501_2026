IntroThe green Sahara

Between roughly 10 000 and 5 500 years ago the Sahara was a grassland dotted with lakes, hippos and people. Then, within a few centuries, it turned to desert. This conceptual model of Liu et al. (2006) has just two variables — annual rainfall P and vegetation cover V — coupled by one feedback. Work through the exercises below and answer each question in your report.

E1No stochastic forcing

Start with the stochastic rainfall forcing switched off (Stoch = 0), exactly as in tab 1_No_Stochastic. Plot annual rainfall P(Vt) against time, then switch to the Vegetation tab and plot V against time.

  1. What do the numbers on the time axis mean?
  2. How does the rainfall vary with time? What climate forcing is represented by the long-term decline in rainfall?
  3. What does it mean for the rainfall variability that the stochastic forcing is switched off?
  4. What causes the sudden reduction in rainfall about 815 years after the start?
  5. Plot the vegetation cover V against time. Describe what happens to it.
  6. Why does the major shift in vegetation cover happen, and why does it happen at that moment in time?
  7. What is the impact of the vegetation shift on P(Vt)? Describe the feedback between V and P(Vt).

E2Stochastic rainfall forcing

Switch to model version 2 (tab 2_Stochastic): the same long-term forcing, but each year now receives a random rainfall anomaly drawn uniformly between 0 and Stoch. Note that the background rainfall Pd0 is halved to 150 mm/yr so the long-term mean rainfall is unchanged.

  1. Compare the rainfall with E1. What is the impact of the stochastic forcing on the interannual variability of P(Vt)?
  2. Compare V with your result without stochastic forcing. What are the three most important differences?
  3. What happened to the feedback between V and P(Vt) — is it stronger or weaker? Explain.
  4. Set Stoch from 300 to 200 and plot again. What has changed in the annual rainfall P(Vt)?
  5. What has changed in the vegetation cover V? Explain the difference with Stoch = 300.
  6. Which model version do you consider more realistic, 1 or 2, and why?

E3Filtered stochastic forcing

Switch to model version 3 (tab 3_Stochastic_filtered). The same random forcing is used, but filtered with a 10-year running mean before it enters the rainfall. Start on the Forcing tab to see both the raw and the filtered series.

  1. Plot both stochastic forcings (raw and filtered) against time. What is the main effect of the filtering on the values of the rainfall forcing?
  2. Plot P(Vt) and V against time. What is the impact of the filtered forcing on the vegetation cover V?
  3. How do you explain the difference between the results of versions 2_Stochastic and 3_Stochastic_filtered?
  4. What happened to the feedback between V and P(Vt) — stronger or weaker than without filtering? Explain.
  5. Change the vegetation feedback coefficient DB from 150 to 50 and plot again. What has changed in P(Vt)?
  6. What has changed in the vegetation cover V? Explain the difference with DB = 150.

E4Unstable vs. stable collapse

Liu et al. (2006) distinguish several types of vegetation decline. In an unstable collapse (UC) the system crosses a bifurcation and the feedback drives it, unforced, to the other state; in a stable collapse (SC) the transition follows the forcing and the noise, without a runaway. The Compare tab overlays the vegetation cover of all three model versions run with your current parameters.

  1. In which experiment and figure(s) did you find an unstable collapse (UC)?
  2. And a stable collapse (SC)?
  3. What is the difference between the two types of collapse?
  4. Which of the two do you consider more likely to have occurred in North Africa during the Holocene, and why?

E5The Sahel under global change

Some climate projections suggest that rainfall in the Sahel/Sahara region will increase under an enhanced greenhouse effect — the forcing runs the other way, and much faster. Use the model to think about that, keeping its assumptions in mind.

  1. Discuss the relevance of the Sahara model for the future vegetation cover of the Sahel/Sahara region. Can the model be used for this purpose?
  2. What climatic processes would you suggest adding to the model to make it more realistic?
  3. What is the main difference between the conditions 6500 years ago and those at the end of the 21st century?
