How much energy do the Sun and the Earth radiate?

One equation answers both — Stefan–Boltzmann. The Sun outshines the Earth by a factor of 3 billion, and yet the two are locked in exact energy balance. Here is why both statements are true.

Recap · what is an electromagnetic wave?

Everything on this page — Planck's law, Wien's law, the greenhouse effect — describes electromagnetic radiation: a self-propagating oscillation of electric and magnetic fields that carries energy through space, even a vacuum, at the speed of light, c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s. Wavelength λ and frequency ν describe the same oscillation from two sides; energy arrives in discrete photons of size hν.

c = λ ν      E = h ν = hc/λ c = 2.998 × 10⁸ m/s · h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J s — the same h and c that build σ, in Section 1

1 · The law

Every object with a temperature radiates

Stefan–Boltzmann gives the radiant flux — power per unit of emitting surface — leaving a body at temperature T:

A↑ = ε σ T4   [W m−2] and for a whole body of area A:   P = A · A↑  [W]
A↑ flux (upward irradiance), W per square metre of surface — Goosse et al. (2010) Eq. 2.1
σ 5.670374 × 10−8 W m−2 K−4
T absolute temperature, kelvin — never °C
ε emissivity, 0–1; ε = 1 is a blackbody
A emitting area (4πR² for a sphere)
P total radiated power, watts

It is not an independent postulate — it is Planck's law, integrated. Integrating the spectral radiance Bλ(T) over all wavelengths gives σT4/π, and integrating that radiance over the outward hemisphere with the cosine projection factor multiplies it back by π:

A↑ = ∫0∞∫2π Bλ(T) cos θ dΩ dλ = σT4,    σ = 2π5kB4 / 15c2h3 σ is built entirely out of kB, h and c — it is not measured independently
The whole story is in that exponent. Flux scales as T4, so a 10 % warmer object radiates 1.104 ≈ 1.46× more, and a body twice as hot radiates 16× more. This is what makes a 5772 K Sun so overwhelmingly brighter than a 255 K Earth, and it is also what makes planetary climate strongly self-stabilising.

2 · Where the energy goes: ψ + α + ζ = 1

Every interface splits radiation three ways

Hit any surface or medium with a beam of radiation and it has exactly three possible fates: some passes straight through, some bounces back, and some is absorbed and turned into heat. The three fractions — each between 0 and 1, and each generally a function of wavelength — always add up to the whole beam:

ψ + α + ζ = 1 ψ transmissivity · α reflectivity · ζ absorptivity — all dimensionless, all functions of λ
ψ transmissivity — fraction of I that passes through
α reflectivity — fraction of I bounced back
ζ absorptivity — fraction of I absorbed and converted to heat

Worked example — a green leaf. Chlorophyll absorbs the visible band hard, for photosynthesis; the leaf's internal cell structure scatters near-infrared light back out almost unused. Same leaf, two wavebands, very different split:

Visible · 0.4–0.7 µm
ζ ≈ 85%
Near-infrared · 0.7–1.3 µm
ψ ≈ 40%
α ≈ 40%
ζ ≈ 20%
ψ transmitted α reflected ζ absorbed

In the visible band ζ dominates — that's photosynthesis at work. In the near-infrared, ζ stays well under 50 %: most of the beam is reflected or scattered straight through the leaf's internal structure, not absorbed. That contrast — dark in the visible, bright in the near-infrared — is exactly what vegetation indices like NDVI detect from satellites.

Two things this connects to, later on this page. First, Kirchhoff's law: at thermal equilibrium a body's absorptivity equals its emissivity at the same wavelength, ζ(λ) = ε(λ) — the same ε from Section 1's Stefan–Boltzmann law. Second, the planetary albedo αp used from Section 6 onward is exactly this α, integrated over the solar spectrum and averaged over the whole Earth–atmosphere system (αp ≈ 0.30). For an opaque surface, ψ = 0, so ζ = 1 − α there — "absorbed" and "not reflected" mean the same thing.

4 · Worked example — the Sun and the Earth

Total solar luminosity, from two numbers

The Sun's photosphere has an effective temperature of 5772 K and a radius of 6.957 × 108 m. It is close enough to a blackbody that ε ≈ 1.

Sanity check on 3.83 × 10²⁶ W. That is about 1013 times humanity's total energy use — the Sun puts out, every second, roughly what human civilisation consumes in 300 000 years. Fusing 4.3 million tonnes of mass into energy per second (E = mc²) is what sustains it.

Total terrestrial emission, from two numbers

Earth's effective emission temperature is Te = 255 K and its radius is R⊕ = 6371 km. Treating it as a blackbody (ε ≈ 1) gives the infrared flux leaving the top of the atmosphere — the same law, run on the same two ingredients, size and temperature.

Same law, 2.6 × 10⁵ times weaker per square metre. P⊕ = 1.22 × 10¹⁷ W is the same number that appears in Section 7's power ladder as "heat radiated by Earth" — identical, by construction, to the sunlight Earth absorbs (Section 5). Unlike the Sun, Earth has no internal power source of this size: it only re-emits what it receives.

5 · Getting from the Sun to the Earth

The same power, spread over a much bigger sphere

Energy is conserved as light travels, but the area it is spread over grows as 4πr². At Earth's orbit (r = 1 AU = 1.496 × 1011 m) the flux has fallen by a factor of 46 000:

S₀ = L☉ / 4πr² = 3.83 × 1026 / (4π · (1.496 × 1011)²) = 1361 W/m² the Total Solar Irradiance (TSI), or solar constant, S₀ — flux on a surface facing the Sun, above the atmosphere (Goosse et al. 2010, §2.1.1)

Note what has not changed: the Sun still emits 3.83 × 10²⁶ W. Earth simply intercepts a laughably small slice of it — a disc of radius 6371 km at a distance of 150 million km catches 4.5 × 10−10 of the total, about one part in two billion.

6 · Earth's energy balance

Absorbs on a disc, emits from a sphere

This is the single step students most often get wrong. Earth intercepts sunlight over its cross-sectional area πR², because sunlight arrives as a parallel beam. It radiates over its full surface 4πR², because thermal emission goes in all directions from the whole globe. Hence the famous factor of 4.

πR²·S₀(1−αp) = 4πR²·σTe4  ⟹  Te = [ S₀(1−αp) / 4σ ]1/4 R cancels — a planet's radiating temperature does not depend on its size. This is Goosse et al. (2010) Eq. 2.2–2.4, with Te the effective emission temperature and αp the planetary albedo

Balance still holds — it just holds at the top of the atmosphere, not at the ground. Stefan–Boltzmann is untouched; only the height at which the balance is struck has moved.

8 · Not just how much — where

The same law, resolved by wavelength

σT⁴ is the area under a Planck curve. Since Wien's law puts the peak at λmax = 2898 µm·K / T, the Sun and the Earth radiate in almost non-overlapping bands — which is precisely why a greenhouse effect is possible at all: the atmosphere is transparent to one and opaque to much of the other.

9 · The fine print

  • ε is rarely exactly 1. Earth's surface runs ε ≈ 0.95–0.99 in the thermal infrared; polished metals can be ε < 0.05, which is why a thermos works. Using T alone without ε is the second most common error after forgetting kelvin.
  • Te is a definition, not a measurement. 5772 K and 255 K are the temperatures of the blackbodies that would emit the observed power. Neither body has a single physical temperature — the Sun's core is 1.5 × 10⁷ K, and Earth's atmosphere spans 180–320 K.
  • Net exchange, not gross emission, drives heat flow. A body at T₁ in surroundings at T₂ nets εσ(T₁⁴ − T₂⁴). Your body radiates ≈ 940 W but nets only ≈ 190 W in a 20 °C room — you are being irradiated right back.
  • The law is for thermal radiation only. It says nothing about conduction, convection or latent heat, which move most of the energy in Earth's lower atmosphere.
  • T⁴ is the planet's thermostat. Any warming raises outgoing flux as T⁴, pushing back toward balance — around 255 K, roughly 3.8 W/m² per kelvin. Climate forcings are quoted in exactly those units for exactly this reason.
