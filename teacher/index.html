EE501 — teacher outline hub

One entry point for the eight lecture running orders. Each lecture keeps its outline inside its own source folder, next to the deck it was built from; this page is the index over them — what each session is built on, when it runs, how the morning is blocked out, and which public pages it drives. The outlines themselves are not duplicated here: follow the link for the actual running order.

How this is organised. The repository is split by what is on the course site, not by what is secret. Everything student-facing is under docs/ and is live at peterhor.github.io/EE501_2026. Everything instructor-facing sits in the root-level lecture-* folders, one per lecture — tracked in the repository, but never served as part of the site. An outline is therefore always at lecture-N-<topic>/LectureN-Teacher-outline.html, in the same folder as its source .pptx, which stays out of the repository entirely. This hub is the only file that knows about all eight.

How to use it during teaching week. Open this page, open the outline for the day, and keep the outline on the second screen. The outlines are self-contained and print cleanly; they carry the block timings, the numbers to put on the board, the questions to throw back at the room, and direct links into the public pages. This page carries only what is useful between sessions: the shape of the week, what each lecture is built on, and what is still missing.

Lecture 1

Components of the climate system

Mon 7 Sep · 10:15–12:00 and 12:15–~15:47 · 11 blocks

Built on The climate system is five components with five different clocks, and almost everything confusing about climate follows from the fact that they run at wildly different speeds. Each component gets the same four questions: what is in it, how much can it hold, how fast does it respond, what does it do to its neighbours. Second thread: this is the only descriptive lecture in the course — every property named here is explained in a later one, so name the later lecture each time.

Lecture 2

The Earth’s energy budget

Tue 8 Sep · 08:15–12:00 · 8 blocks

Built on The planet must radiate away exactly what it absorbs — and everything in the lecture is that statement applied to a different box: the whole planet gives 255 K, a layer of atmosphere gives the greenhouse effect, a circle of latitude gives the circulation, the globe today gives +0.6 W m−2. Second thread: the greenhouse effect is not a blanket, it is a change of emission altitude.

Lecture 3

Climate at a local scale

Tue 8 Sep · 12:15–16:00 · 6 blocks

Built on The same sunlight falls on every surface. What happens next — how warm it gets, how much water it evaporates, how deep the heat penetrates, how cold it goes at night — is decided by the properties of the surface, not by the radiation arriving. Each section takes one property (albedo, water availability, thermal admittance, roughness, vegetation structure) and shows which term of the budget it controls.

Lecture 4

Global cycles: water and carbon

Wed 9 Sep · 08:15–12:00 · 7 blocks

Built on Every cycle is the same equation — dS/dt = in − out — applied to a different box. What distinguishes reservoirs is not the equation but the residence time, and residence time decides whether a perturbation is a blip or something the planet keeps: nine days of water in the atmosphere, three thousand years in the ocean, no chemical sink for CO2, one for CH4. Second thread: both cycles are enormous fluxes that very nearly cancel.

Lecture 5

Modelling the climate system

Thu 10 Sep 08:15–12:00 + Mon 14 Sep 10:15–12:00 · 8 blocks

Built on A climate model is equations plus a way of solving them — and almost everything that goes wrong lives in the second half. The equations are settled physics; the grid, the time step and the parameterisations are choices, and every disagreement between models is a disagreement about those choices. Second thread: the shift from “what does the climate system do?” to “how would you find out, given that you cannot experiment on the planet?”

The only two-slot lecture. One outline covers both sittings — Blocks 1–4 on the Thursday, Blocks 5–6 and the close on the Monday. It carries separate Session A and Session B headings plus a board plan and an if-time-runs-short section. Check the split before the Monday slot, since any Thursday overrun lands there.

Lecture 6

Response of the climate system to perturbations

Tue 15 Sep · 08:15–12:00 · 8 blocks

Built on The climate does not respond to a forcing — it responds to a forcing plus its own reaction to it, and the second part is two to three times larger than the first. The whole lecture is one calculation carried across four pages: 3.7 → λf → ΔT → ΔT(t). A student who can reproduce 3.7 / 1.26 ≈ 2.9 K and explain every symbol has the lecture.

Opens on a debt from Lecture 5 — the 1.1 K and 3 K left on the board on the Thursday. Block 3, the feedback assembler, is the one to protect; Block 6 is the one to cut into.

Climate change: past and present

Climate change: the future

Thu 17 Sep · 08:15–12:00 · 4-311B · 9 blocks

Built on The physics of the future is better understood than the future itself, because the largest uncertainty is a choice nobody has made yet — and the one number that survives every pathway is the total amount of carbon. Where the outlines and teaching pages depart from the book — most visibly in Lecture 6, which uses the AR5 values ΔQ = 3.7 W m−2 and λ0 = −3.2 rather than the book’s 3.8 and −3.8 — the outline says so at the point where it matters.

What is public, and what is not

There are now two boundaries rather than one, and they do not sit in the same place. Worth knowing before renaming anything:

  • The published site is docs/, and only docs/. That folder is the GitHub Pages root. This hub and the outlines sit outside it, so they are not on peterhor.github.io/EE501_2026 and are not linked from the landing page. A file placed inside docs/ is on the site the moment it is pushed, regardless of its name.
  • The repository is public, and the outlines are in it. Being outside docs/ keeps material off the course site; it does not make it private. Anyone who opens the repository on GitHub can read this hub and all eight running orders, reveal answers included. Write them on that assumption.
  • The outlines are un-ignored by one negation. .gitignore still carries *Teacher*.html as the catch-all for lab answer keys, with !lecture-*/Lecture?-Teacher-outline.html after it to let the running orders through. Keep an outline’s filename in that shape and it stays tracked; give a new answer key a Teacher name and it stays out.
  • Decks, answer keys, screenshots and uploads are still excluded — by the explicit .pptx path list and by the *Report*, *FASIT*, */screenshots and */uploads rules. Lecture 8’s Slide cues point into a deck that is deliberately not reproduced anywhere public.

Check before pushing. git status --short --untracked-files=all should show outlines and this hub, and never a deck, an answer key or anything under uploads. If it shows one of those, a rule stopped matching — fix the rule rather than committing and deleting afterwards, since a committed file stays in the history.
